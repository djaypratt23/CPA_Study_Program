## (b) Lesson design (aud-code-of-conduct)

- **Big idea.** The what, why and example are clear and well motivated. However, the example teaches a wrong rule: it says an inherited interest "impairs regardless", but under ET 1.240.040 independence is not impaired if the interest is disposed of within 30 days (finding 001, S2).
- **Pre-question (aud-coc-pre1).** It primes the right concept: an immediate family member's direct interest impairs at any amount. The stem needs "on the engagement team".
- **Body.**
  - Order: Code structure, then the conceptual framework (seven threats, safeguards), covered members, financial interests and loans, family, nonattest services, and fees and other rules. The order is logical.
  - The worked example (Harbor Bank) is correct. It shows the two-question method: first ask whether the person is a covered member, then whether a specific rule applies.
  - The tables are accurate.
- **Weak spots in the body.**
  - The close-relative scope is overstated (finding 010).
  - The lesson says nothing on the 5% rule for non-covered partners, gifts, insured deposits, immediate-family employment and benefit plans, the contingent-fee exceptions, valuation and FIS design services, or unsolicited interests (finding 012).
  - The recruiting row needs a key-position caveat (finding 004).
- **Key takeaways.** These are accurate and consistent with `aud-high-yield.md`. The "leases" wording is loose (finding 021).
- **Inline checks.** chk1 tests threat classification and chk2 tests the nonattest general requirements. Both are well placed.
- **Minutes.** About 1,460 words including the frontmatter (about 900 in the body), plus 2 checks, 1 worked example and 5 tables. A realistic time is 12 to 18 minutes for a rule-dense lesson, so the stated 18 is acceptable, if generous.
- **Lesson–item alignment.** These items test material the lesson never teaches:
  - aud-coc-26 and aud-x1-04: the 5% rule
  - aud-coc-14: gifts
  - aud-x1-01 and aud-x1-26 (d): deposits at a client bank
  - aud-coc-12 and aud-coc-24: immediate-family employment and benefit plans
  - aud-x1-05: court-fixed fees
  - aud-x1-25 and aud-coc-07 (b): the tax-examination exception
  - aud-coc-01 (c): self-directed retirement accounts
  - aud-tbs-r3-nonattest s5 and s6: FIS design and valuation
  - aud-tbs-r3-nonattest s2 and aud-coc-06 (d): payroll transmission conditions
  - aud-tbs-u1-independence s2: inherited interests

  In addition, aud-tbs-r3-conduct-rules v5 (advertising) is only named in the Part 1 table. The SEC/PCAOB rows of aud-tbs-x1-independence (s4–s7) are taught in the next module, aud-sec-pcaob-independence. That is acceptable for an exam-pool TBS mapped to both modules.
- **Sequencing.** This is the first module in AUD. It relies on nothing taught later, apart from the SEC rows in the exam TBS noted above. NOCLAR (ET 1.180, effective June 30, 2023) is not covered here; it appears in aud-written-representations and the predecessor TBS. Consider adding a one-line pointer.

## (c) Practice design

- **Counts.** The module has 27 practice MCQs plus 3 lesson MCQs, 9 exam MCQs, 7 flashcards, and 3 practice TBS plus 1 exam TBS. This is adequate for the topic weight (Area I is 15–25%).
- **Difficulty spread (practice and exam, 36 items).** By label, 9 are difficulty 1, 17 are difficulty 2 and 10 are difficulty 3. Several of the difficulty-3 labels are inflated recall items: aud-coc-15, -18, -26 and -27. After the relabeling proposed in findings 006 and 007, 13 are difficulty 1, 21 difficulty 2 and 2 difficulty 3 (aud-coc-11 and aud-x1-04). Genuinely hard items are scarce outside the TBS.
- **Progression.** The practice order is mixed rather than easy to hard. Items 18–27 are terse, short-stem items labeled analysis or evaluation, though they are among the easiest in the set.
- **Skill mix.** The labels give remembering 10, application 18, analysis 5 and evaluation 3. On my judgment it is remembering 14, application 22, analysis 0 and evaluation 0 (findings 006 and 007). This module is mostly recall and application, which suits the Area I ethics tasks. The inflated labels overstate the section's analysis and evaluation share, however.
- **Key letters.** Across the 36 practice and exam MCQs: c 13, a 9, d 9, b 5. The skew is mild.
- **Redundancy.** Unpaid fees appear in 3 MCQs, 2 TBS rows and a flashcard. Contingent fees appear in 4 MCQs and a TBS row. Self-review is tested twice in the same way. Five exam items nearly duplicate practice items (findings 013 and 014).
- **Misses route back.** Every item carries moduleId aud-code-of-conduct, so a miss routes the learner to this lesson.
