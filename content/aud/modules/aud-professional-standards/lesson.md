## Who sets which standards?

| Engagement | Entity | Standards | Issued by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audit | Nonissuer (private company, NFP) | GAAS — AU-C sections | AICPA Auditing Standards Board (ASB) |
| Audit | Issuer (SEC registrant) | PCAOB auditing standards — AS | PCAOB (approved by the SEC) |
| Audit of a government or federal award recipient | Governmental | Government Auditing Standards (Yellow Book) + GAAS | GAO |
| Examination, review, or agreed-upon procedures on subject matter | Any | SSAEs — AT-C sections | ASB |
| Preparation, compilation, review of financial statements | Nonissuer | SSARS — AR-C sections | AICPA Accounting and Review Services Committee |
| Audit under international standards | Any | ISAs | IAASB |

## What an audit promises (and doesn't)

| The auditor **does** | The auditor **does not** |
|---|---|
| Obtain reasonable assurance about material misstatement from fraud or error | Guarantee the statements are accurate or that fraud is absent |
| Exercise professional skepticism and judgment | Prepare the statements or maintain internal control |
| Plan and perform the audit to detect material misstatements | Detect immaterial misstatements |
| Report the opinion (and communicate certain matters) | Assure the entity's future viability |

**Inherent limitations** make absolute assurance impossible: judgment in estimates, sampling, evidence that is persuasive rather than conclusive, and fraud that is concealed through collusion or forgery.

## The premise of an audit

The audit is conducted on the premise that **management** (and, where appropriate, those charged with governance) acknowledges and understands its responsibility for:

1. preparing and fairly presenting the statements under the applicable framework;
2. designing, implementing, and maintaining internal control relevant to that preparation; and
3. providing the auditor with access to all relevant information, additional information requested, and unrestricted access to people.

These acknowledgments are obtained in the engagement letter and confirmed in the written representation letter.

## Reading the standards: "must" vs. "should"

```worked
title: How binding is the requirement?
scenario: |
  A standard says the auditor "should" perform a procedure. In this engagement, the auditor believes an
  alternative procedure achieves the same objective.
steps:
  - label: What category is "should"?
    work: Presumptively mandatory requirement
    result: Comply in all cases where relevant, unless an exception is justified
  - label: Can the auditor depart?
    work: Only in rare circumstances, with alternative procedures that achieve the requirement's objective
    result: Yes, but the justification must be documented
  - label: What if the standard said "must"?
    work: Unconditional requirement
    result: No departure is permitted
insight: Explanatory material ("may", "might", "could") gives guidance but imposes no requirement.
```
