bigIdea:
  what: >-
    The auditor reports to those charged with governance (usually the audit committee) on its responsibilities, the
    planned scope and timing, and significant findings — difficult judgments, disagreements, uncorrected
    misstatements, and more. It's a two-way conversation that helps the board oversee management.
  why: >-
    The board can't oversee what it doesn't know. Many of the things auditors see — pressure on estimates,
    disagreements with management, a second opinion sought from another firm — are exactly what governance needs
    to hear.
  example: >-
    Management pushed back hard on the auditor's inventory reserve and consulted another accounting firm. The
    auditor tells the audit committee about the disagreement (even though it was resolved) and about the
    consultation.

keyTakeaways:
  - "Communicate: the auditor's responsibilities; an overview of planned scope and timing; and significant findings."
  - "Significant findings: qualitative aspects of significant accounting practices (policies, estimates, disclosures); significant difficulties; disagreements with management (even if resolved); uncorrected misstatements (and material corrected ones); significant issues discussed with management; written representations requested; management's consultations with other accountants; other significant matters."
  - "Form: significant findings are communicated in writing when oral communication wouldn't be adequate. Significant deficiencies and material weaknesses are always in writing (AU-C 265)."
  - "Timing: on a timely basis — planning matters early; significant difficulties possibly as they occur."
  - "Issuers (AS 1301) add communications such as critical accounting policies and estimates, and independence (Rule 3526)."
  - "If the two-way communication is inadequate, the auditor evaluates the effect on the risk assessment and its ability to obtain evidence."

## What to communicate

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Auditor's responsibilities | Opinion on the statements; reasonable assurance; the audit doesn't relieve management of its responsibilities |
| Planned scope and timing | Overview, significant risks identified — without compromising the audit's effectiveness |
| Significant findings | Qualitative aspects of accounting practices; significant difficulties; disagreements; uncorrected misstatements; representations requested; consultations with other accountants; other significant issues |
| Internal control | Significant deficiencies and material weaknesses, in writing |

## Form and timing

| Matter | Form |
|---|---|
| Significant findings | In writing when oral communication wouldn't be adequate |
| Significant deficiencies and material weaknesses | Always in writing — by the report release date, and no later than 60 days after |
| Planning matters | Oral or written, before significant fieldwork |
