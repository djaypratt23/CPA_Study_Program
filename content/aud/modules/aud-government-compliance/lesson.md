## GAGAS engagements

| Engagement | What it adds |
|---|---|
| **Financial audit** under GAGAS | GAAS plus: a report on internal control over financial reporting and on compliance with provisions of laws, regulations, contracts and grants (no opinion on either), reporting of findings with the views of responsible officials, and GAGAS independence and CPE requirements |
| **Attestation engagement** under GAGAS | AT-C plus GAGAS reporting |
| **Performance audit** | Assesses program effectiveness, economy and efficiency, internal control or compliance — no counterpart in GAAS |

GAGAS findings to report include significant deficiencies and material weaknesses, fraud and noncompliance with a material effect, and noncompliance with contracts or grants that warrants the attention of those charged with governance.

**GAO independence** uses a conceptual framework like the AICPA's. The auditor may not assume **management responsibilities**. Before providing a nonaudit service (such as preparing the statements), the auditor must conclude that management has the skills, knowledge or experience to oversee it, and must document that assessment.

## Single audits (Uniform Guidance)

A non-federal entity (state or local government, NFP, college, tribe) that **expends $1,000,000 or more** of federal awards in a year needs a **single audit** (or, if it has only one program, sometimes a program-specific audit).

1. The auditee prepares a **schedule of expenditures of federal awards (SEFA)**.
2. The auditor identifies **Type A** programs (above a threshold — at least $1,000,000) and **Type B** programs, assesses risk, and selects **major programs** covering at least **40%** of expenditures (**20%** for a low-risk auditee).
3. For each major program, the auditor obtains an understanding of internal control over compliance, **tests those controls**, and tests compliance with the applicable **compliance requirements** (e.g., allowable costs, eligibility, reporting) using the OMB **Compliance Supplement**.
4. **Questioned costs** above $25,000 (known and likely) for a type of compliance requirement for a major program are reported as findings.

The reporting package goes to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse within the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's reports or **nine months** after year-end.

## Compliance audits and compliance attestation

- **AU-C 935** applies when a GAAS audit is combined with a law or grant requiring an opinion on compliance, as in a single audit. Materiality is set for each **program** (or compliance requirement), not for the financial statements as a whole.
- **AT-C 315** covers stand-alone engagements to report on compliance with specified requirements or on internal control over compliance: an **examination** (opinion), a **review** (compliance only — not internal control over compliance), or **agreed-upon procedures**.

## Employee benefit plan audits (ERISA) and DOL independence

Audits of ERISA plans follow AU-C 703. When the plan administrator elects an ERISA section 103(a)(3)(C) audit, investment information certified by a qualified institution is not audited, and the report describes the scope and the certified information. The **Department of Labor** independence rules are stricter than the AICPA's in some respects: the firm and its members may not have **any direct financial interest** or a material indirect interest in the plan or plan sponsor, and may not serve as promoter, underwriter, investment adviser, trustee, officer or employee of the plan or sponsor.
