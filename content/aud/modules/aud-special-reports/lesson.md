  what: >-
    Not every audit is of GAAP statements. Auditors also report on cash-basis or tax-basis statements, on a single
    element such as rent revenue, and on compliance with a loan agreement. Each report adapts the standard format
    so readers know exactly what was audited and on what basis.
  why: >-
    A cash-basis statement that looks like a GAAP balance sheet could mislead. Special titles, an emphasis
    paragraph about the basis, and use restrictions keep readers from assuming more than the report says.
  example: >-
    A small partnership prepares tax-basis statements for its lender. The audit report gives an opinion "in
    accordance with the basis of accounting the Partnership uses for income tax purposes" and includes an
    emphasis-of-matter paragraph noting that basis differs from GAAP.

keyTakeaways:
  - "Special purpose frameworks (AU-C 800): cash basis, tax basis, regulatory basis, contractual basis, and other bases with substantial support."
  - "The report expresses an opinion in accordance with the special purpose framework and includes an emphasis-of-matter paragraph alerting users to the basis of accounting. Regulatory and contractual bases generally require an other-matter paragraph restricting use."
  - "Statement titles must not suggest GAAP (e.g., \"statement of assets and liabilities arising from cash transactions\", not \"balance sheet\")."
  - "Single statements or specific elements (AU-C 805): materiality is set relative to the element; the report identifies the element and framework."
  - "Compliance with aspects of contractual agreements (AU-C 806): negative assurance (\"nothing came to our attention…\") given in connection with an audit, in a separate report or an other-matter paragraph; use is restricted."
  - "Comparative statements: the opinion covers each period audited. If a predecessor audited the prior year and its report isn't reissued, an other-matter paragraph describes it. If the prior year wasn't audited, an other-matter paragraph says so."
  - "If the auditor's updated opinion on a prior period differs from the one previously expressed, an other-matter paragraph explains why."

## Special purpose frameworks

| Framework | Example | Restricted use? |
|---|---|---|
| Cash basis | Small professional firm | No |
| Tax basis | Partnership reporting to owners | No |
| Regulatory basis | Insurance company under state rules | Yes, generally (if also general purpose, no) |
| Contractual basis | Statements required by a purchase agreement | Yes |

  - label: Use restriction
    work: Tax basis
    result: Not required

## Comparative statements

| Prior-year situation | Report treatment |
|---|---|
| Audited by the same auditor | Opinion covers both years |
| Audited by a predecessor; report not reissued | Other-matter paragraph: prior year audited by other auditors, date and type of their opinion (and reasons if modified) |
| Not audited (reviewed or compiled) | Other-matter paragraph stating the level of service and that no audit opinion is expressed on the prior year |
| Updated opinion differs from the prior report | Other-matter paragraph explaining the change |

## Other special engagements

- **Compliance with contractual agreements:** given with an audit; negative assurance; restricted to the parties to the agreement.
- **Letters for underwriters (comfort letters):** negative assurance on unaudited interim information and changes after the audited statements; addressed to the underwriters and the client.
- **Review of interim financial information (AU-C 930):** limited assurance based on inquiry and analytics, by the entity's annual auditor.
