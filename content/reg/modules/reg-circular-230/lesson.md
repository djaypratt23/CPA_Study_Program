## Who may practice before the IRS?

| Practitioner | Representation rights |
|---|---|
| CPAs, attorneys, enrolled agents | Unlimited — any matter, any taxpayer |
| Enrolled actuaries, enrolled retirement plan agents | Limited to their specialty |
| Annual Filing Season Program (AFSP) participants | Limited — examinations of returns they prepared and signed |
| Other paid preparers (PTIN only) | May prepare returns; no representation rights |

## Key Circular 230 duties

| Section | Rule |
|---|---|
| §10.21 Knowledge of omission | Advise the client promptly of the error and its consequences |
| §10.22 Due diligence | Exercise diligence in preparing returns and making representations |
| §10.27 Fees | No unconscionable fees; contingent fees only in the listed exceptions |
| §10.28 Client records | Return records needed for the client's compliance, even in a fee dispute |
| §10.29 Conflicts of interest | Informed written consent from each affected client (within 30 days) |
| §10.30 Solicitation | No false, fraudulent, or coercive advertising |
| §10.34 Returns | Don't sign a return with a position lacking a reasonable basis, an unreasonable position, or a willful or reckless understatement |
| §10.35 Competence | Have the knowledge, skill, and preparation needed |
| §10.37 Written advice | Base it on reasonable assumptions and relevant facts |

## Contingent fees: the exceptions

A practitioner may charge a contingent fee only for services in connection with:
1. an IRS **examination** or challenge to an **original** return;
2. an **amended** return or claim for refund filed within **120 days** after receiving a written notice of examination or challenge;
3. a claim solely for **statutory interest or penalties**; or
4. a **judicial proceeding** under the Internal Revenue Code.

## AICPA Statements on Standards for Tax Services

- **Tax return positions:** comply with the taxing authority's standards; don't recommend a position that exploits the audit selection process or is frivolous; advise the client about potential penalties and disclosure.
- **Answers to questions on returns:** make a reasonable effort to answer all questions; explain omissions.
- **Procedural aspects:** may rely in good faith on client information but not ignore implications; make reasonable inquiries if information looks incorrect or incomplete.
- **Estimates:** may use reasonable estimates when records aren't available, if not implying greater accuracy than exists.
- **Errors discovered:** inform the client promptly and recommend corrective action; don't inform the taxing authority without permission (except where required by law).
- **Written advice:** communicate in writing when appropriate; no obligation to update advice for later law changes unless agreed.
