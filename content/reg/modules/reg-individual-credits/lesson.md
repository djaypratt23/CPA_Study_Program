bigIdea:
  what: >-
    Credits reduce tax dollar for dollar — more valuable than a deduction of the same amount. Refundable credits can
    even create a refund beyond the tax owed; nonrefundable credits stop at zero tax.
  why: >-
    Families and students often qualify for several credits, each with its own rate, cap, and income phase-out.
    Picking the right one (and not double-dipping) is a common exam and real-world task.
  example: >-
    A married couple with two children under 17 and $160,000 of AGI claims $4,400 of child tax credit — two
    children × $2,200 — with no phase-out, since AGI is below $400,000.
preQuestions: [reg-cr-pre1]
keyTakeaways:
  - "Child tax credit (2025): $2,200 per qualifying child under 17 with an SSN; refundable portion (additional child tax credit) up to $1,700 per child. Phase-out: $50 per $1,000 (or part) of MAGI over $200,000 ($400,000 joint)."
  - "Credit for other dependents: $500 per dependent who isn't a qualifying child for the CTC (e.g., a 17-year-old or a dependent parent); nonrefundable."
  - "Child and dependent care credit (2025): 20%–35% of expenses up to $3,000 (one qualifying person) or $6,000 (two or more); 35% at AGI of $15,000 or less, falling 1 point per $2,000 to 20% above $43,000; expenses limited to the lower-earning spouse's earned income; nonrefundable."
  - "American opportunity credit: up to $2,500 per student (100% of the first $2,000 + 25% of the next $2,000) for the first 4 years of postsecondary education; 40% refundable. Lifetime learning credit: 20% of up to $10,000 per return ($2,000); nonrefundable. Both phase out at MAGI $80,000–$90,000 ($160,000–$180,000 joint). Only one per student per year."
  - "Refundable: earned income credit, additional child tax credit, 40% of the AOTC, premium tax credit, excess Social Security withheld. Nonrefundable: the non-refundable CTC portion, other dependents, dependent care, lifetime learning, saver's, foreign tax credit, residential energy credits."
  - "Energy credits: under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, the residential clean energy and energy efficient home improvement credits end for expenditures after 2025, and the clean vehicle credit ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025."
citations:
  - source: IRC §24 (Child tax credit), §21 (Dependent care), §25A (Education credits), §32 (Earned income credit)
  - source: Public Law 119-21 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, 2025)

## Family credits

```worked
title: Child tax credit with a phase-out
scenario: |
  The Nguyens (MFJ) have three children aged 4, 9, and 17, and MAGI of $412,500 for 2025.
steps:
  - label: Children qualifying for the $2,200 CTC (under 17)
    work: Ages 4 and 9
    result: 2 × 2,200 = 4,400
  - label: Credit for other dependents
    work: The 17-year-old
    result: 500
  - label: Phase-out
    work: MAGI over 400,000 = 12,500 → 13 increments of $1,000 (round up) × $50
    result: 650
  - label: Total credit
    work: 4,900 − 650
    result: 4,250
insight: The phase-out applies to the combined CTC and other-dependent credit, and any partial $1,000 counts as a full increment.
```

## Child and dependent care credit

| AGI | Credit rate |
|---|---|
| $15,000 or less | 35% |
| $15,001–$43,000 | Drops 1 point per $2,000 |
| Over $43,000 | 20% |

Qualifying expenses: care for a child under 13 or a disabled spouse or dependent so the taxpayer (and spouse) can work — limited to $3,000/$6,000 and to the lower-earning spouse's earned income. Employer dependent care benefits excluded from income reduce the expense limit.

## Education credits

| | American opportunity (AOTC) | Lifetime learning (LLC) |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum | $2,500 per student | $2,000 per return |
| Computation | 100% of first $2,000 + 25% of next $2,000 | 20% of up to $10,000 |
| Years | First 4 years of postsecondary; at least half-time | Unlimited years; any courses to improve job skills |
| Refundable | 40% (up to $1,000) | No |
| Phase-out (MAGI) | $80,000–$90,000 single; $160,000–$180,000 joint | Same |
