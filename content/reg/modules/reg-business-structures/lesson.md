keyTakeaways:
  - "General partnership: no filing needed; each partner is an agent of the partnership; partners are jointly and severally liable for partnership obligations. Profits are shared equally unless agreed; losses follow profits."
  - "Limited partnership: a certificate is filed with the state; at least one general partner (unlimited liability); limited partners' liability is limited to their investment."
  - "LLP: partners are shielded from personal liability for other partners' malpractice and (in most states) partnership debts, but remain liable for their own negligence."
  - "LLC: articles of organization filed; members have limited liability; member-managed or manager-managed; taxed as a partnership by default (single-member: disregarded)."
  - "Corporation: articles of incorporation filed; shareholders have limited liability (unless the veil is pierced); the board of directors manages and appoints officers."
  - "Promoters are personally liable on preincorporation contracts unless a novation releases them; the corporation becomes liable when it adopts the contract."
  - "Directors and officers owe fiduciary duties of care and loyalty; the business judgment rule protects good-faith, informed decisions."
  - "Shareholders vote on directors and fundamental changes (mergers, sales of substantially all assets, amending the articles, dissolution). Dissenting shareholders may have appraisal rights. Shareholders may bring derivative suits on the corporation's behalf."

## Comparing entities

| | General partnership | Limited partnership | LLP | LLC | Corporation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formation | No filing | Certificate filed | Filing | Articles of organization | Articles of incorporation |
| Owner liability | Unlimited (joint and several) | GPs unlimited; LPs limited | Shielded from others' malpractice | Limited | Limited |
| Management | All partners equally | General partners | Partners | Members or managers | Board of directors → officers |
| Default federal tax | Partnership | Partnership | Partnership | Partnership (disregarded if one member) | C corporation (S if elected) |
| Transfer of interest | Only financial rights transfer without consent | Same | Same | Same | Freely transferable shares |

## Partners' rights and duties

- Each partner can bind the partnership in the ordinary course (apparent authority).
- Unanimous consent is needed for extraordinary acts (admitting a partner, amending the agreement, selling substantially all assets).
- Partners owe duties of loyalty and care; no salary for services unless agreed (except winding up).
- A new partner is liable for pre-existing debts only up to the capital contributed; a dissociated partner remains liable for debts incurred before dissociation.

## Corporations

```worked
title: Who is liable?
scenario: |
  Four situations at Birch Corp.
steps:
  - label: A promoter signed an office lease before the corporation existed; the corporation later adopted the lease
    work: Adoption makes the corporation liable, but the promoter isn't released without a novation
    result: Both the corporation and the promoter are liable
  - label: The sole shareholder commingles personal and corporate funds and undercapitalizes the company to defraud creditors
    work: Grounds to pierce the corporate veil
    result: The shareholder can be personally liable
  - label: Directors approve an acquisition after thorough analysis; it later fails
    work: Business judgment rule — informed, good faith, no conflict
    result: Directors not liable
  - label: Directors approve a dividend that makes the corporation insolvent
    work: Illegal distribution
    result: Directors who voted for it are personally liable to the corporation
insight: Limited liability is the rule; the exceptions arise from misconduct, missing formalities, or personal guarantees.
```

## Fundamental changes

Mergers, consolidations, sales of substantially all assets outside the ordinary course, amendments to the articles, and dissolution require **board approval and shareholder approval**. Shareholders who dissent from a merger or asset sale may demand **appraisal** (payment of the fair value of their shares).
